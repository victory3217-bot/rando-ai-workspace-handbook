# CH03. What You Can Enter into AI, and Information Security

> **Note.** This handbook is updated continuously. Product screens and policies can change, so check the sources and verification dates in each chapter.
>
> **How to read.** Text without a label is a general explanation. What was confirmed in official product documentation is collected in **Official documentation check** boxes with a source number (for example `[S1]`); the source URL and check date are in the table in section 9. Classifications and judgments that this book's author made for teaching purposes are marked **Author's interpretation**. If a table looks cut off on a phone, swipe it sideways.
>
> **Please read first.** This chapter is not legal advice or security consulting. If your company has an information security policy or AI usage guidelines, *those come before this chapter*. The exercises in this chapter use **fictional data and file names only**. **There is no exercise in this chapter that enters real personal information or customer data into an AI service**, and this chapter does not ask you to enter your company's non-public work material into an AI service either. For your own real work material, you decide the tier *by looking only at the name and type*. Do not enter material that your organization has not approved.

## 1. Learning Objectives

When you finish this chapter, you will be able to do the following.

- Explain that *transmission*, *retention*, and *training use* are three separate questions about what you enter into an AI service.
- Sort work material into *information you may enter / information you enter only after masking / information you do not enter*, and give the reason.
- Set a habit of never entering personal information, sensitive information, passwords, or API keys into an AI service.
- Know how to check how the AI services you use (ChatGPT, Claude, and others) handle data, using the official documentation and your own account settings screen.
- Know the difference between a personal account and an organizational account, and ask the person in charge which account to use for work.
- Create fictional data and practice safely.
- Review candidate task A, which you chose in CH01 and CH02, and its material using this chapter's tiers, and decide what to do before entering anything into AI (masking, checking with the person in charge, not entering).

## 2. Core Concepts

**Terms used in this chapter**
- **Model training**: When an AI service company uses what users wrote as material to make its AI better. Official documentation also calls this "improving the model."
- **API key**: A long string that works like a password when a program connects to an AI service. Anyone who knows it can use the service as your account.
- **Personal / organizational account**: An account you sign up for on your own is a personal account. An account that a company or school uses with an administrator (official documentation calls these Business, Enterprise, Edu, `Claude for Work`, and similar names) is an organizational account. The exact names can change with the service and over time.
- **De-identification**: Changing information so it is hard to tell whose it is.
- **Personal information**: Information that can identify a living person. This book uses the term in its general sense; the legal definition and scope may differ.

### 2-1. Transmission, retention, and training are separate questions

Most AI services send the text or files you enter to the service company's systems for processing. This is called **transmission**. What can happen after transmission can be split into three separate questions.

| Question | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Is it transmitted? | Does my input arrive at the service company? | An AI service that runs on a server generally does this |
| Is it retained? | How long do records such as chat history remain? | Differs by service, account type, and settings |
| Is it used for training? | Can the company use my content to improve its model? | Differs by service, account type, and settings |

> **Author's interpretation** Common misunderstandings are "They said they don't use it for training, so it's safe" and "They said it isn't saved, so it's fine to send." Even if it is not used for training, transmission still happens, and even if it is not retained, transmission still happens. You decide what to enter *only after checking all three separately*. Section 3 looks at a case in which one public repository states this distinction at the very beginning of its document.

### 2-2. Information tiers: three groups

> **Author's interpretation** The tiers below are a *teaching scheme* that the author made so that general office workers can decide quickly. **They are not a legal classification or an official standard.** Ask your company's information security or legal contact whether a given piece of information counts as personal information or a trade secret under the law.

| Tier | Name | Criterion | Examples | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Information you may enter | Already public, or not tied to any specific person or company | An introduction posted on the website, the public version of a press release, a general-knowledge question, this book's fictional data | You may enter it |
| B | Information you enter after masking | *Internal* company work information that is left with only its structure once names, amounts, and contact details are removed | Meeting minutes, draft internal reports, terms by client | Enter it **only in a service and account your organization allows**, and only after masking the parts that could identify someone |
| C | Information you do not enter | Information that is hard to take back once sent, or that is likely to need protection under a contract or rule | Lists that tie together information that can identify people, information generally treated as sensitive, passwords and API keys, confidential material, material your organization prohibits | Do not enter it |

**Typical items in Tier C (the author's examples)**
- Information that can identify a person: a customer list that ties names to contact details, numbers such as a national ID number, bank account numbers
- Information generally treated as sensitive: health information, private matters such as a person's beliefs or the groups they belong to (this may differ from the legal scope of "sensitive information")
- Means of access: passwords, verification codes, API keys, notes that list an access address together with an account
- Material covered by a contract's confidentiality clause (a promise not to disclose the contract's contents to outsiders), and a business plan before it is released to investors

### 2-3. How AI services handle and retain data: what the official documentation confirms (reference study)

What follows was compiled on 2026-10-26 by reading the official help pages and privacy pages directly in a browser. Policies and screens change often, so **before you rely on this section as it is, check it again against your own account screen and the current official documentation.** Anything the documents do not state was not assumed.

> **Official documentation check — ChatGPT (OpenAI), checked on 2026-10-26**
>
> - In the personal services (Free, Plus, Pro), a user's content may be used for model training. To turn this off, turn off `Improve the model for everyone` under Settings > Data controls in ChatGPT, or choose `Do not train on my content` in the Privacy Portal. Either one is enough. `[S1]`
> - The Data controls document states that this setting is on by default for personal accounts (Free, Plus, Pro). `[S2]`
> - Even with this setting off, if you send feedback on a response, such as a thumbs up or down, the entire conversation linked to that feedback may be used for training. `[S1]` `[S2]`
> - Turning off the training setting does not delete or hide saved conversations. You must delete conversations or your account separately. `[S2]`
> - While in the temporary state, a Temporary chat does not appear in your history, does not create or change memory (a feature in which the AI remembers content), and is not used to improve the model. However, it may be kept for up to 30 days for safety purposes. If you save it, it becomes a regular chat and follows your account settings. `[S1]` `[S2]`
> - ChatGPT Business, Enterprise, Edu and the API by default do not use inputs and outputs to improve the model. `[S1]` `[S2]`

> **Official documentation check — Claude (Anthropic), checked on 2026-10-26**
>
> - In the personal consumer products (Claude Free, Pro, Max, and Claude Code, the developer tool used with those accounts), conversations may be used in these cases: (1) the user allowed their use for model improvement, (2) the conversation was flagged for safety review, or (3) the user otherwise explicitly took part in training. Whether to allow it is set under `Model Improvement` in Privacy Settings. `[S3]`
> - Incognito chats are not used for model improvement even when `Model Improvement` is on. `[S3]`
> - If you send feedback with a thumbs up or down, the entire related conversation may be kept for up to 5 years. `[S3]`
> - When you delete a conversation, it disappears from your history immediately and is deleted from the service company's servers (the document's wording is "backend storage") within 30 days. `[S4]`
> - If you allow model improvement, data may be kept in a de-identified form in the model training process for up to 5 years (this applies to new or resumed conversations after you allow it). If you turn the permission off, earlier and new conversations are not used for future training, but data already in a training run in progress or in an already trained model remains. `[S4]`
> - For conversations flagged by automated systems for violating the usage policy, inputs and outputs may be kept for up to 2 years. `[S4]`
> - Work products (Claude for Work, the Anthropic API, and similar) by default do not use inputs and outputs for model training. The exceptions are when feedback is sent or the user has given permission. `[S5]`

**What this chapter did not confirm (not assumed)**
- The documents I read do not say what the *default* value of `Model Improvement` is for Claude's personal products. Check the current state directly on your own account settings screen.
- I did not read the *conversation retention periods* or the administrator-setting details for ChatGPT Business, Enterprise, and Edu, or for Claude for Work. The OpenAI documentation only says that a managed workspace may have organization-level retention, memory, and access settings `[S2]`. Ask your organization's administrator.
- I did not check detailed differences by plan, differences by region, or changes after the time you read this.

### 2-4. Personal accounts and organizational accounts (reference study)

> **Official documentation check — defaults of work products, checked on 2026-10-26**
>
> - Both companies state in their documents that their work (organizational) products by default do not use inputs and outputs for model training. `[S1]` `[S2]` `[S5]`

> **Author's interpretation** That does not mean "anything may be entered if it is an organizational account."
>
> - Even in an organizational account, your company's rules decide *which material you may enter*.
> - Entering company work material into a *personal account* may violate company rules. First ask which services and which accounts your company allows.
> - If it is a personal account, you must check the training setting and how to delete conversations yourself (Exercise 4 in section 5).
> - If you cannot see a settings menu in an organizational account, it may be because an administrator manages it. The OpenAI documentation says the available settings can differ depending on the account type and workspace settings `[S2]`.

### 2-5. Protecting passwords and API keys

Passwords and API keys are *keys*. Sending text that contains a key to someone else is the same as handing them the key.

> **Author's interpretation** Habits to keep (general security recommendations, not the official guidance of any particular service)
>
> 1. Do not paste passwords, verification codes, or API keys into an AI service. When you paste an error message or a settings file, first check whether a key is mixed into it.
> 2. Do not leave a key in a file name, the title of a note app entry, or a screenshot.
> 3. Keep passwords in a password manager, and turn on two-step verification (a method that asks for one more check, such as phone verification, in addition to the password) for the services that offer it.
> 4. If you suspect you entered a key somewhere, *treat it as already exposed*, revoke it immediately in that service, and create a new one. (Each issuing service has different screens, so follow that service's instructions.)

### 2-6. Creating safe fictional data

For practice or demonstrations, use material that has *the same structure but fake content* instead of real material.

| What to change | How to change it |
|---|---|
| Person names | Labels such as Gana, Ramaba, or Contact A |
| Company names | Example Trading Co., Company A, Company B |
| Amounts and quantities | Rough numbers that differ from the real ones |
| Contact details | `010-0000-0000`, `example@example.com` |
| Dates | A rough period or a fictional date |
| Addresses, accounts, numbers | Leave them out or mark them `○○○` |

Even after masking, it must not be possible to *guess whose story it is*. Sometimes the content itself identifies someone, such as a unique incident that happened at only one company in the industry.

## 7. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | How to fix it |
|---|---|---|
| The menu names on my screen differ from the document | Screens can differ by plan, app version, region, and organization settings | Look at the document's check date, and give priority to the descriptions on your own screen. If you are not sure, write "Don't know" and ask the person in charge. Do not insist it is as the document says when the screen and the document differ |
| It is an organizational account, but there is no training setting menu | The available settings can differ depending on the account type and workspace settings `[S2]`. An administrator may be managing it | Ask the administrator about the retention period and whether training use applies |
| I already entered sensitive content | I used it before deciding the tier | Do not hide it; follow this order. ① If it is organization material, tell the person in charge ② Delete that conversation in that service — turning off the training setting alone does not delete saved conversations `[S2]`. Claude explains that a deleted conversation disappears from your history immediately and is deleted from the service company's servers within 30 days `[S4]` ③ If it was a password or API key, revoke it immediately in that service and create a new one |
| I turned off the training setting but I am still uneasy | There are exceptions such as feedback | Remember that sending feedback such as a thumbs up or down may lead to that conversation being used for training or long-term retention `[S1]` `[S3]`. Do not send feedback in conversations with sensitive content |
| I masked it but I can still tell whose story it is | The content itself identifies someone | Generalize even the features of the incident (timing, region, amount), or treat that material as C |
| I cannot decide which tier it is | The criterion is unclear | Choose the more conservative tier and ask the person in charge. Do not enter it until it is confirmed |
